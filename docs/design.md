# Long Light: design

Approved by the owner on 2026-11-06. "My call" marks decisions the owner delegated.

## Intent

A relaxing fishing game that is first of all beautiful. The owner wants the most aesthetic sunsets,
walks to and from a boat, different situations at different times of day, and nothing else that
demands effort. They should never have to code or do anything manual.

## Goals

- Painted storybook look: every frame reads as brushwork on canvas.
- A small cove: cottage on the hill, path through grass and pines, jetty, rowboat, calm bay, small
  islands, layered blue mountains.
- Walk down, step into the boat, row anywhere, cast, wait, gentle bite, reel in. Row back, walk home,
  or sit and watch.
- A day takes about 30 real minutes. The clock can be paused or jumped to any time.
- Dawn, day, sunset (the centrepiece), dusk and night each look, sound and fish differently.
- Painted journal of fish found.

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- No failure of any kind: no timers, money, score, lost line.
- No busy HUD. No grinding. Nothing that makes the player hurry.
- Fails if it looks like a filter over grey boxes, or if sunsets are merely orange.

## Constraints

- Hardware: RTX 4070 SUPER, 240 Hz 1440p primary. Hardware is not the bottleneck.
- All art and sound generated by code. No paid services, no downloaded art.
- Double-click launcher in `Downloads\claude\long-light`, chromeless browser window, works offline.

## Decisions (my call)

- Three.js with custom GLSL, TypeScript, Vite, bundled to a single HTML file.
- Third-person camera. Keyboard, mouse and PS4 controller.
- Sky: physically based atmosphere plus raymarched clouds, time-sliced into a panorama so it can use
  high sample counts.
- Water: real planar reflection, wave spectrum rebaked each frame, ripple simulation for wakes.
- Paint: anisotropic Kuwahara filter, flow-aligned bristle relief, canvas weave.
- West is the open bay, so the sun sets over the water when walking down to the jetty.
- Game opens in late afternoon so the first walk leads into the sunset.

## Playing together

One player hosts and everyone else connects to them over WebRTC (PeerJS, with its public service for the introductions). The host passes each player's state on to the others 15 times a second and owns the clock, so everyone shares one sky. Nothing is simulated twice: a friend's fisher and boat are puppets driven by what they send. All incoming data is cleaned in `src/net/protocol.ts` before the game uses it.

A window that is covered or minimised stops drawing, so a one-second timer keeps the clock and the connection going until it comes back.
